# Bench rubric

Two pages built from the same prompt are compared on each criterion below. For each one, choose the page that does it better, or tie when the difference is too small to name. Judge only what the images show. Whether the page works (contrast failures, keyboard focus, overflow, console errors, speed) is measured by scripts and scored separately, so it decides nothing here.

A page wins a criterion on evidence you can point to in an image, not on a general impression. Polish that serves nothing in the prompt counts for nothing; a plain choice made well beats an ambitious one made badly.

## first_screen

The desktop and phone first screens. Within a few seconds, does a reader know what this is, who it is for and what to do next? Is there one clear focal point, a deliberate hierarchy, and a primary action that stands out without shouting? Does the phone first screen keep that, or does it lose the action or the message below the fold?

## whole_page

The whole page and the three scroll depths. Does each section have a job the previous one did not do, in an order that builds a case? Is the rhythm varied on purpose (dense against open, wide against narrow) rather than a stack of identical bands? Do spacing and alignment hold across the page? Does the ending close the page, or does it just stop?

## imagery

Everything the page shows other than text: illustrations, product screens, diagrams, data, shapes, built-in-code objects. Is it specific to this subject, believable, and made with care at the size it is shown? Does it carry information or atmosphere the text cannot? Stock-like filler, generic gradients and decorative blobs count against a page; no imagery at all is better than imagery that says nothing.

## type

Choice of typefaces, scale, weight and measure. Does the type suit the subject's voice? Is there a clear scale with real contrast between levels? Are line lengths and line spacing comfortable at both widths? Are headlines set with care (breaks, tracking, widows) rather than left to fall?

## motion

The four frames after navigation show what moves in the first three seconds; the scroll depths show whether content waiting to be revealed has appeared. Does motion direct attention to something that matters, or does it delay the content? A page whose first screen is still blank or half-faded at 1.5 s loses this criterion. A still page ties with one whose motion adds nothing.

## fit

Fit to the subject. Could this page belong to a different product or organisation with the words swapped, or is every major choice (colour, type, imagery, layout, tone of copy) traceable to something in the prompt? Does the copy use the prompt's facts, and invent none that matter?

## overall

Which page would you rather ship for this prompt, weighing everything above? This is not a count of the criteria won: one decisive failure can outweigh several small wins.
