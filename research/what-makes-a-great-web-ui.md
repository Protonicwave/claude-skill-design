# Great interfaces decide what slop leaves to chance

The best-looking websites look good because almost nothing on them is left to default. Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Family and the Awwwards winners each work from a small, computed system (colour generated from a few perceptual inputs, one typeface used with conviction, a constrained spacing scale, depth taken from surface tone rather than ornament) and then spend their boldness on one idea: Stripe's gradient wave, Apple's scroll-scrubbed product shots, Igloo Inc's real-time 3D world. Underneath the look sits functional discipline that is measurable rather than mystical: visible navigation, feedback inside 100 to 400 ms, motion under 300 ms that disappears entirely on frequent actions, Core Web Vitals passed at the 75th percentile, and WCAG 2.2 AA. Even Awwwards, the home of spectacle, weights design and usability at **70% of a site's score** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)). "AI slop" is the mirror image: the statistical median that fills every decision a person did not make, which is why **22% of recent Show HN sites trip four or more of the same tells** (Inter, indigo gradients, a pill above a centred hero, three icon cards, emoji icons, coloured glows) ([Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). The practical lesson is that beauty is a process, not a palette: ground every choice in the subject, write the system down before building, budget emphasis and motion by frequency, then critique the result against the generic default and delete whatever does not serve. Ban lists on their own fail, because a model told to avoid Inter simply drifts to the next most probable font.

## Acclaimed sites compute their look from three inputs, not ninety-eight

The companies behind the most admired interfaces barely talk about aesthetics. They talk about systems, contrast and tooling, and the look falls out of those. Linear's 2024 redesign is the clearest case. Each theme previously required **98 hand-set variables**; the team moved theme generation from HSL to LCH, a perceptually uniform space in which lightness 50 reads as equally light across every hue, and cut the inputs to **three: base colour, accent colour and contrast** ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). The same generator produces every elevation surface (background, panels, dialogs, modals), so depth is expressed as lightness steps rather than shadows, and the contrast input yields accessible high-contrast themes for free. Linear also deliberately capped how much blue fed into its chrome for "a more neutral, timeless look", and its March 2026 refresh moved further, from cool blue greys to a warmer, less saturated grey, while warning that going too warm reads as "muddy" ([Linear](https://linear.app/blog/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). Vercel's Geist system takes the same stance with tokens: "monochrome by default; colour only for meaning, always paired with a non-colour cue", Geist Sans for prose and controls, Geist Mono for code, paths, timestamps and identifiers, weights loaded from **400 to 600 only**, a 12, 6 and 4 column grid across desktop, tablet and mobile, and the blunt rule "Shadows: none. Ornamental shadows and fake depth are rejected" ([vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)). That file is an agent-facing guide for generated pages, so it is stricter than Vercel's own marketing homepage, but it states the house philosophy plainly.

### Colour: mostly grey, one accent, everything derived in OKLCH

The reproducible version of Linear's approach is OKLCH. Because perceived lightness stays constant across hues, a hover state is the same colour with lightness raised by 0.1, a darker variant is `oklch(from var(--accent) calc(l - 0.1) c h)`, and an error colour can be made by swapping only the hue (`oklch(from var(--error) l c 140)`) so it keeps contrast parity with the accent ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl)). All major browsers now support both `oklch()` and relative colour syntax. Radix supplies the missing discipline about where each value goes: a **12-step scale in which every step has a job**, with step 1 the app background, steps 3 to 5 component backgrounds and their hover and selected states, step 6 subtle borders on non-interactive elements, steps 7 and 8 interactive borders and focus rings, step 9 the solid accent, and steps 11 and 12 text guaranteed to reach APCA Lc 60 and Lc 90 against step 2 ([Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)). Refactoring UI's widely summarised principle completes the picture: almost all of an interface is grey, so build eight to ten greys, start from a very dark grey rather than black, and tint them slightly cool or warm ([Refactoring UI summary](https://sglavoie-github-io.pages.dev/posts/2023/09/09/book-summary-refactoring-ui/)). The working palette is therefore one tinted neutral scale, one accent scale and three or four semantic scales, each used by role rather than by eye.

Contrast is set by perception, not habit. APCA puts the preferred floor for body text at **Lc 90**, the minimum for body columns at Lc 75, other content text at Lc 60, headlines at Lc 45, and anything below Lc 15 as effectively invisible ([APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)). APCA is more reliable than WCAG 2.x ratios on dark themes, but WCAG 2.2's 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text and UI components remains the legal standard, so a serious build checks both ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). Dark mode is a separate palette, not an inversion: Material recommends **#121212** rather than black as the base surface, lighter surfaces for higher elevation, and desaturated accents that pass AA at every level ([Material](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme)). Gradients look cheap for a specific, fixable reason: interpolating two saturated colours in RGB averages the channels into a "grey dead zone", and writing `linear-gradient(to right in oklch, yellow, blue)` routes the blend through green and aqua instead ([Josh W. Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/make-beautiful-gradients/)). A low-opacity SVG `feTurbulence` noise layer over the gradient removes banding and adds the tactile grain that trend pieces now favour over costly WebGL depth ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/?p=351302)).

### Typography: one family with a point of view, tuned by numbers

Butterick's rules are still the baseline: body text **15 to 25 px**, line spacing **120 to 145%** of the size, a measure of **45 to 90 characters**, bold and italic used as little as possible and never together, and 5 to 12% extra tracking on all caps ([Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html)). Line height should fall as size rises; Tailwind's defaults encode this, running from 1.5 at 16 px to 1.2 at 30 px and 1.0 at 48 px and above ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/font-size)). Tracking should tighten with size too: Rasmus Andersson's published curve for Inter gives roughly zero at 12 px, about -0.011em at 16 px and about -0.022em from 32 px upwards ([Inter dynamic metrics](https://d.rsms.me/inter-website/v3/dynmetrics/)). Scale should be fluid rather than stepped by breakpoint: Utopia defines one modular scale for a small screen (18 px base, ratio 1.2) and another for a large one (20 px base, ratio 1.25) and lets `clamp()` interpolate between them ([Utopia](https://utopia.fyi/blog/designing-with-fluid-type-scales)). Two CSS properties now fix most ragged wrapping for free: `text-wrap: balance` on headings and `text-wrap: pretty` on paragraphs, both Baseline since 2024 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/text-wrap); [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-text-wrap-pretty)). APCA ties weight to size as well: at Lc 75, 16 px body needs weight 500 and 14 px needs 700, so thin display weights are a contrast decision, not just a stylistic one ([APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)).

The acclaimed product sites keep type narrow and confident. Linear uses Inter Display for headings and regular Inter elsewhere ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)); Vercel bans arbitrary sizes, numeric weights and all-caps eyebrow labels ([vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)); Teenage Engineering uses monospace across site, products, packaging and social as a deliberate identity ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/teenage-engineering)). The choice of face matters more than it used to. Practitioners now describe Inter, Roboto, SF Pro and Helvetica as a fatigue signal, a complaint about homogeneity rather than quality ([Font Alternatives](https://fontalternatives.com/blog/neo-grotesk-fatigue-2026/)). Linear and Vercel can use a neo-grotesque because everything around it is decided; a site that adopts Inter without that system inherits only the sameness.

### Space, depth and detail come from scales, not judgement in the moment

Spacing follows a constrained scale on a 4 px base (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 and upwards), and proximity carries grouping: closer elements read as more related ([Refactoring UI summary](https://sglavoie-github-io.pages.dev/posts/2023/09/09/book-summary-refactoring-ui/)). Vercel makes the rule explicit with within-group gaps of tokens 2 to 4, between-group gaps of 6 to 8, section turns of 8 to 12, and 16 reserved for "a true chapter break" ([vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)). The useful heuristic is that the gap between groups should be at least double the gap within one, and that generous section spacing (64 px or more on desktop) is the cheapest visible difference between amateur and polished marketing pages.

Depth in this cohort comes from luminance steps and hairline borders, and Linear's 2026 principle names the goal: "Structure should be felt, not seen", which meant softening border contrast and removing separators ([Linear](https://linear.app/blog/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). Where shadows are used, they follow physics. Josh Comeau's recipe is one global light source, several layered low-opacity shadows that double in offset and blur, and shadow colour tinted with the background hue rather than transparent black, which looks washed out on coloured surfaces ([Josh W. Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/)). Nested corners follow the concentric rule: inner radius equals outer radius minus padding, so a 24 px card with 8 px padding holds a 16 px child ([30 Seconds of Code](https://30secondsofcode.org/css/s/nested-border-radius)). Glass, when it is justified, needs an extended backdrop: naive `backdrop-filter: blur()` samples only the pixels directly behind the element and looks flat, so the blur goes on a child twice the header's height, masked, with a translucent tint and an opaque `@supports` fallback ([Josh W. Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/backdrop-filter/)). Raycast is the instructive exception to tone-only depth: its keycap gradients and inset highlights model a literal physical object, the keyboard, which is why they read as meaningful rather than decorative ([open-design.ai](https://open-design.ai/systems/raycast/)). Icons need the same consistency: one 24 px grid, one 2 px stroke, round joins and caps, matched optical weight ([Lucide](https://lucide.dev/guide/design/icon-design-guide)). Mixing icon families, radii or shadow recipes is among the fastest ways to make a page look assembled rather than designed.

## Hierarchy comes from dimming everything except the content

The second shared trait is that the content is the brightest thing on the page and every other element is made quieter. Linear's 2026 refresh states it as a principle, "Don't compete for attention you haven't earned": the sidebar went "a few notches dimmer", the tab bar became compact with smaller icons and text, and coloured team icon backgrounds were removed, all to keep information density "without the interface feeling overwhelming" ([Linear](https://linear.app/blog/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). Vercel tells its page generator to establish "the reader's question and strongest supported answer before layout", treats emphasis as scarce, and rejects pills and badges for ordinary metadata, cards nested in cards and decorative icon tiles ([vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)). Family's wallet rejects "Everything is right there, all the time, whether you need it or not" in favour of showing fundamentals and revealing features as they become relevant ([Family](https://benji.org/family-values)). Stripe's January 2026 homepage began with Patrick Collison asking what a website is for; the answer was a bento grid that presents billing, usage-based pricing and tax as one integrated suite, with interactive modals revealing detail progressively and a live counter of the share of global GDP running on Stripe as proof ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/katie-dill-stripes-homepage-redesign-reflects-its-growth-78-of-forbes-ai-50-rely-on-its-products-and-the-importance-of-clarity-in-web-design-y-combinator-startup-podcast/); [YouTube summary](https://youtube-transcript.ai/docs/how-stripe-built-their-new-website-xjvghrn4pb)). The pattern these sites share reads as claim, proof, action: one statement, one real product view or live number, one call to action. Subtracting contrast from secondary areas is cheaper and more effective than enlarging the primary one.

Navigation is where restraint must not tip into hiding. The Nielsen Norman Group's study of 179 participants found that on desktop, hidden menus were used in **27%** of cases against **48%** for visible navigation, that hidden navigation cut content discoverability by **more than 20%**, and that it made desktop tasks **at least 39% slower** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). The recommendation is direct: show top-level links on desktop, show up to four visibly on mobile, and use a menu only for the overflow. Sticky headers should be small, opaque rather than translucent, and barely animated; a partially persistent header that slides back in over 300 to 400 ms on upward scroll is the best compromise, and NN/g cites a 13:1 content-to-chrome ratio as reasonable against a wasteful 2:1 ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sticky-headers/)). Expert accelerators sit on top of visible navigation rather than replacing it: a Cmd+K palette should be "the one place where users can find every command", fuzzy-match on keywords and synonyms, and display each command's shortcut so it teaches itself ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-to-build-a-remarkable-command-palette/)). The mechanics matter as much as the layout. Filters, tabs and pagination belong in the URL so refresh, share and Back work; navigation uses real links so middle-click and Cmd+Click work; Back restores scroll position; and anchored headings carry `scroll-margin-top` so a sticky header never covers them ([Vercel guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Jakob's Law explains why: users expect a site to behave like the sites they already know ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)), and a beautiful page that breaks the Back button reads as broken.

## Motion earns its place by frequency, origin and one big idea

### Everyday motion is short, fast-starting and often absent

The respected practitioners agree on a narrow grammar. Emil Kowalski's rules are that UI animations should usually run **under 300 ms**, use a strong ease-out because it starts fast and feels responsive, animate only `transform` and `opacity` (padding, margin and width trigger layout and paint), stay interruptible, and fall back to a simple fade under `prefers-reduced-motion` ([Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)). A 180 ms select animation feels more responsive than a 400 ms one; buttons scale to 0.97 on press; nothing should ever animate in from `scale(0)`; and popovers should grow from their trigger using the component library's transform-origin variable rather than from the centre ([Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)). His sharpest rule is "never animate keyboard-initiated actions", because they repeat hundreds of times a day. Rauno Freiberg reaches the same place from interaction design: command menus, context menus and the app switcher appear instantly, and he removed the animations from his own bookmarking tool after they felt sluggish over days of use ([Rauno](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)). He adds the physical properties that make motion feel native: interruptibility, gestures that apply their delta immediately rather than playing a canned animation after a threshold, lightweight actions that commit mid-gesture while destructive ones wait for the finger to lift, and spatial consistency, so that elements launch from their source.

Family turned spatial consistency into its signature: "We fly instead of teleport." Trays rise from the element that summoned them, tabs slide in the direction of the tapped tab, wallet cards move between screens rather than duplicating, and "Continue" morphs into "Confirm" by reusing shared letters ([Family](https://benji.org/family-values)). Family also states the rule that reconciles spectacle with restraint: "Mastering delight is mastering selective emphasis." Confetti appears only after a wallet backup, and sound only on the trash action. Motion is budgeted by frequency, and that single rule explains both Raycast's total lack of animation and Family's confetti. Vercel sits at the still end of the range, defaulting to stillness and banning bounce, parallax, scroll-triggered reveals, marquees and pulsing indicators in its documentation register ([vercel.com/design.md](https://vercel.com/design.md)). A defensible token set follows: 100 to 150 ms for hovers, presses and tooltips, 150 to 250 ms for dropdowns and popovers, 250 to 400 ms for modals and page transitions, and up to 500 ms only for large gesture-driven sheets such as Vaul's drawer with `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` ([Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-drawer-component)). Native View Transitions, Baseline for same-document use since 2025, now give multi-page sites shared-element continuity without a single-page framework ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API); [OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/frontend-trends-shaped-web-2025/)).

### Spectacle wins awards only when it carries one idea and holds frame rate

At the top of the award circuit, real-time 3D dominates. Awwwards Site of the Year went to **Lusion v3** in 2023, **Igloo Inc by abeto** in 2024 and **Lando Norris by OFF+BRAND** in 2025, and Developer Site of the Year matched or went to abeto in each of those years ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards/winners); [Awwwards Sites of the Year](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_year/)). A small group of specialist studios (Immersive Garden, Resn, Lusion, abeto, The First The Last, Louis Paquet) collects most of the monthly honours, and their own studio sites and brand sites win often because those briefs carry maximum creative freedom and minimal conversion pressure ([Awwwards Sites of the Month](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_month/)). The default technical stack is GSAP with Lenis smooth scroll, scroll-driven storytelling, and Three.js or WebGL in the ambitious entries, with intro animations, kinetic type and custom cursors recurring across 2025 winners ([Orpetron](https://orpetron.com/blog/design-legends-of-2025-showcasing-the-best-websites-of-the-year/)). WebGPU is the rising frontier, with studio sites now running entirely on it ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/10/20/from-garage-to-browser-forged-build-and-the-webgpu-revolution/)).

What separates winners from imitators is not the stack. Awwwards weights **Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20% and Content 10%**, sends each site to at least 18 jurors and discards the three scores furthest from the mean, which structurally favours coherent polish over polarising experiments ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)). Hon Tran, an Awwwards juror, says jurors reward "one strong idea carried through", an idea that could not be moved to another brand, art direction where "the static frames still look intentional without motion", and smooth frame rates on mid-range devices; jank, desktop-only thinking, effects without a concept and missing reduced-motion handling "typically cap a site in the mid-7s" ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria); [Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). The jury's praise for Igloo Inc names the balance exactly: an immersive 3D experience paired with easy scroll navigation ([Awwwards SOTY 2024](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards-2024/site-of-the-year)). The counterexample proves that spectacle is optional: CSS Design Awards gave **Dropbox Brand by Daybreak Studio both Website of the Year 2025 and Best UX** for a systematised, legible brand-guidelines site ([CSSDA](https://www.cssdesignawards.com/blog/2025-website-of-the-year-winners/430/)).

The best product sites isolate their spectacle in a GPU-rendered layer so the rest of the page stays cheap. Stripe's 2020 hero gradient ran on "minigl", a minimal WebGL wrapper with custom shaders, and its 2026 site keeps a signature wave refined over years ([Bram.us](https://www.bram.us/2021/10/13/how-to-create-the-stripe-website-gradient-effect); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/katie-dill-stripes-homepage-redesign-reflects-its-growth-78-of-forbes-ai-50-rely-on-its-products-and-the-importance-of-clarity-in-web-design-y-combinator-startup-podcast/)). Apple's scroll reveals, as reconstructed by the community, map scroll progress to a frame index and draw one image per frame to a `<canvas>` (147 frames for the AirPods Pro hero), with a single fallback image served on constrained devices ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/?p=308477)). The cost of getting this wrong is concrete: one agency reports that a single Spline hero adds **800 kB to 2 MB of JavaScript** and that heavy `backdrop-filter` blur costs 15 to 30% of frame rate on mid-tier Android ([StudioMeyer](https://dev.to/studiomeyer_io/web-design-trends-2026-what-actually-held-up-after-six-months-23p8)), figures that come from its own client work rather than an independent benchmark. The criticism of award culture is fair: Greenspector found an Awwwards Mobile Excellence nominee with many accessibility errors and a very high usability score, and a studio insider on Hacker News said flashiness mattered far more than performance there ([Greenspector](https://blog.greenspector.com/en/analysis_sites_nominated_mobile_excellence_awwwards/); [Hacker News](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/44221430)). Both are dated or anecdotal, and no systematic 2024 to 2026 audit of winners' Core Web Vitals exists, so the honest position is that award spectacle is a craft benchmark, not a usability benchmark.

## Functional polish is measurable, and it is where most sites lose

The polish people describe as "feel" is mostly a dense set of small, checkable rules. Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines are the most complete public checklist ([Vercel guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Spinners and skeletons wait **150 to 300 ms** before appearing and, once shown, stay for at least **300 to 500 ms**, so fast actions show only the result and slow ones never flash. Updates are optimistic: the interface changes immediately, reconciles with the server, and rolls back with an error or offers Undo on failure. Destructive actions require confirmation or a safe Undo window. Hover, active and focus states carry more contrast than rest. Labels are specific ("Save API Key", not "Continue"), errors explain the fix, skeletons match the final layout exactly, and every screen offers a next step, with empty, sparse, dense and error states all designed. Forms keep inputs at 16 px or more to stop iOS zooming, never block paste, keep submit enabled until submission begins, place errors beside fields and move focus to the first one. The first tooltip in a group waits; its neighbours then open instantly with no animation. NN/g sets the loading thresholds: no indicator under one second, a skeleton for full-page loads of two to ten seconds, a progress bar with an estimate beyond ten, and no shimmering skeletons, which distract and raise accessibility concerns ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)). The Peak-End Rule from Laws of UX adds a priority: people judge an experience by its peak and its ending ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)), so completion states and confirmations deserve as much polish as the hero.

Speed is the most underrated aesthetic property. Interactions under **0.1 s** feel instantaneous, one second preserves flow of thought, and ten seconds is the limit of attention ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)); the Doherty Threshold puts the productivity cliff at about **400 ms** ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/)). Core Web Vitals set the public floor: **LCP at most 2.5 s, INP at most 200 ms and CLS at most 0.1**, measured at the 75th percentile and passed only if all three pass ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). INP counts every click, tap and key press, so a heavy hero that blocks the main thread fails the whole page ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/inp)). Layout shift is mostly preventable: set image dimensions, reserve space for late content, match fallback font metrics and animate with transforms ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/cls)). Fonts should be WOFF2, subset, with only a few critical files preloaded, an explicit `font-display` value and `size-adjust` on the fallback ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)). Speculation Rules can prefetch eagerly and prerender on hover, and combined with cross-document View Transitions they give multi-page sites near-instant, animated navigation ([HTMHell](https://htmhell.dev/adventcalendar/2025/3/)). Aim for a visible response within 100 ms through pressed states and optimistic changes, and treat INP's 200 ms as the floor rather than the goal.

Accessibility is part of quality, not a separate track, and WCAG 2.2 encodes several things that also make an interface feel better. Focus must not be entirely hidden by sticky headers or overlays (2.4.11), every drag needs a single-pointer alternative (2.5.7), and pointer targets must be at least **24 by 24 CSS px** (2.5.8) ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Vercel's practical target is 44 px on mobile, with checkbox labels sharing the hit area and no dead zones ([Vercel guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Every focusable element shows a visible `:focus-visible` ring, flows work entirely by keyboard, semantic HTML comes before ARIA, icon-only buttons have accessible names, and colour never carries meaning alone. Spectacle stays accessible through a simple split: WCAG 2.3.3 does not count colour and opacity changes as motion animation and treats parallax as non-essential ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)), so flourishes built on opacity, colour and blur can stay for everyone while translate, scale and parallax sit behind `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. Functional gaps are also a slop tell in their own right: AI-built forms routinely ship without validation, error states, required-field indicators or accessibility, because models learn from static code rather than from use ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)).

## AI slop is the median filling every unmade decision

### The tells are catalogued and measurable

The tells of AI-generated frontends are now documented in near-identical form by Anthropic, independent designers, agencies and one measured study. Adrian Krebs scored roughly 1,400 to 1,590 recent Show HN submissions with deterministic DOM and computed-style checks (no screenshots or model judgement, an estimated 5 to 10% false-positive rate) and found **22% triggered four or more patterns, 32% triggered two or three, and 46% triggered none or one** ([Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). Anthropic's own Applied AI team says an unguided model "almost always" converges on Inter, purple gradients on white and minimal animation, and its current frontend skill adds the hero formula of "a big number, a small label, supporting stats and a gradient accent", single-word accent styling, numbered markers for things that are not sequences, and decorative dividers that carry no information ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills); [Anthropic SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). Jeff Humble's seven tells add emoji as icons, cards nested in cards, multicoloured side tabs on every card and status dots detached from any real status ([Fountain Institute](https://pages.thefountaininstitute.com/posts/7-tells-that-a-ui-is-ai-generated)). A parallel critique targets the human-made SaaS template that preceded AI: dark slate surfaces with a faint violet or cyan accent, a blurred coral-to-violet mesh across the top third of the page, a monospace accent word in the headline, a Cmd+K pill in the corner and a "trusted by" logo row at 60% opacity ([Brainy Papers](https://brainy.ink/paper/why-every-saas-looks-the-same-2026)). That source claims the dark template covers "roughly 70%" of new B2B launches but gives no methodology, so the number is colour rather than evidence.

The tells cluster into five layers, and a fix that addresses only one leaves the others intact.

| Layer | Typical tells | What the acclaimed sites do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Inter everywhere; second-wave substitutes Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, Geist; one italic serif, mono or gradient accent word in the headline | One face chosen for the subject, a display cut or tight tracking for headings, hierarchy from size and weight steps ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)) |
| Colour | `indigo-500` buttons, purple-to-blue gradients, aurora glows behind the hero, coloured box-shadows, neon on dark, mid-grey body text that barely passes | Tinted neutrals, one accent carrying meaning, gradients only where they model something, contrast checked at Lc 75 or more ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design.md); [APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)) |
| Structure | Centred hero with a pill above the H1, three identical icon cards, numbered 1, 2, 3 steps, stat banner row, logo row, cards in cards | Claim, proof, action; progressive disclosure; layout derived from the content rather than three equal columns ([Family](https://benji.org/family-values); [925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)) |
| Micro-ornament | Emoji icons, status dots everywhere, coloured side borders, all-caps eyebrow labels, decorative dividers, glassmorphism | Borders, labels and numbers only where they carry information; one icon family ([Anthropic SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md); [Lucide](https://lucide.dev/guide/design/icon-design-guide)) |
| Copy and function | "Build faster. Ship smarter."; forms with no validation, error, empty or loading states; no focus rings | Specific copy naming the audience; every state designed; WCAG 2.2 AA as a floor ([925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells); [Vercel guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)) |

### Why models and templates converge

Anthropic names the mechanism "distributional convergence": safe choices that "work universally and offend no one" dominate web training data, so a model left without direction samples from that centre ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Path dependence sharpens it. Tailwind's creator Adam Wathan joked in August 2025 about having made every Tailwind UI button `bg-indigo-500` five years earlier, and commentators trace the purple default to that demo choice being copied until it read as "modern web design" ([DEV](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p); [prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)); the causation is plausible and widely asserted but unproven. A feedback loop then tightens variance: AI tools learn from shipped products that already share the template, and their output feeds the next crawl ([Brainy Papers](https://brainy.ink/paper/why-every-saas-looks-the-same-2026)). Sameness also predates AI. Daryl Ginn named "the Linear effect" in January 2023 and called Stripe's 2020 site "the old Linear" ([Ginn](https://rectangle.substack.com/p/the-linear-effect)), and critics fault imitators who copy the dark-glow surface while losing "the discipline that made the original work" ([webtrends](https://webtrends.pages.dev/posts/linear-landing-page-breakdown)). The decisive evidence about remedies is Anthropic's own admission that banning defaults only displaces them: told to avoid common fonts, the model drifted to Space Grotesk, which then appeared on Krebs's list of "LLM font combos" ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills); [Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). Several of the "distinctive" fonts Anthropic recommended in 2025 are tells by 2026.

### Remedies that survive the next mode

Because the tell set mutates, durable guidance forces decisions rather than listing forbidden values. Anthropic's current skill moved from a ban-and-recommend list to a process: identify the subject, audience and primary job; draw visual choices from the subject's industry and vernacular; open the hero with the most characteristic element of that world; write a compact plan (four to six colours, type roles, a layout concept, alignment and principles); review the plan against the brief and revise anything that reads as a generic default; build to a quality floor of responsiveness, visible focus and reduced motion; then critique screenshots and remove decoration that does not serve ([Anthropic SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). Concrete direction steers hard: Anthropic's example of a named fantasy theme (parchment, leather, ornate borders, medieval serifs) produced output entirely unlike the default, and a typography mandate alone improved other aspects of the design ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Supplying tokens and requiring the model to use only those names stops it inventing `bg-indigo-500` ([DEV](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p)), which is also why Vercel positions v0's design-system import as its answer to an overused default look ([Vercel Community](https://community.vercel.com/t/theming-in-v0/17000)).

Distinctiveness comes from provenance. Build the palette from something true about the product, choose a typeface with a point of view, break the three-card triptych with content that does not fit three equal columns, and write copy that names the audience ([925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)). Pick a fight with one or two tells rather than all of them, keep the template's useful parts (clear type, strong hierarchy), carry the identity into the dashboard, loading states and onboarding, and apply the swap test: if a stranger could replace your wordmark with a competitor's unnoticed, the design has not been made yet ([Brainy Papers](https://brainy.ink/paper/why-every-saas-looks-the-same-2026)). Restraint is itself distinctive in a field of glows: at most one deliberate gradient, one dominant accent, and status dots only for real status ([Fountain Institute](https://pages.thefountaininstitute.com/posts/7-tells-that-a-ui-is-ai-generated)). The sceptics deserve a hearing. Krebs calls the vibe-coded look "not really bad, just uninspired", and LogRocket notes there is "no real evidence" that the Linear style performs better ([Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design)). No source in this research links any aesthetic to conversion. The case for distinctiveness rests on brand and trust, and on Anthropic's own observation that generic output makes an interface "easy to dismiss".

## A build sheet of defaults that protect good decisions

Defaults are only slop when they are unexamined. The values below are well-sourced starting points that leave the identity decisions (typeface, accent hue, art direction, the one big idea) free for the subject to determine.

| Dimension | Default | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Body text | 16 to 20 px, line height about 1.5, measure about 65 characters within 45 to 90 | [Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html) |
| Headings | Line height 1.0 to 1.2, tracking about -0.02em at 24 px and above, `text-wrap: balance` | [Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/font-size); [Inter metrics](https://d.rsms.me/inter-website/v3/dynmetrics/); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/text-wrap) |

The sites that set these standards enforce them through process rather than taste alone. Linear's 2024 redesign ran in five milestones behind a feature flag with an internal toolbar toggle between old and new UI, so regressions were judged side by side, and its 2026 refresh used a colour tool built with Claude Code inside the dev toolbar whose tokens were exported as JSON into Figma, keeping design and code identical ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui); [Linear](https://linear.app/blog/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). Stripe's team describes resisting "good enough" thinking so that every decision is intentional, and used AI image generation and prototyping to widen exploration rather than to replace judgement ([YouTube summary](https://youtube-transcript.ai/docs/how-stripe-built-their-new-website-xjvghrn4pb)). Comparison, a single source of truth for tokens and a refusal to ship the first plausible answer are the mechanisms; the beauty is their output.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "What makes a great web UI?" and "What makes AI slop?" have the same answer seen from opposite sides: slop is not a style but the absence of decisions, and great UI is the presence of decisions that can each be traced to the subject, the user or the physics of perception. That is why a site can use Inter and look exceptional (Linear) while another uses an "interesting" font and still looks generated: the test is not which value was chosen but whether anything chose it. The most useful single check for any element is whether it could belong to any product. If it could, it is either a deliberate, well-tuned default from the build sheet or it is slop; there is no third category.

Two shifts follow for anyone building now, with or without AI. First, the frontier has moved towards calm. Linear's warmer, dimmer 2026 interface and Vercel's stillness rules show the originators retreating from the dark-glow look as it became the imitators' signature, while award work splits between immersive real-time 3D and quiet, type-led editorial systems such as Dropbox Brand. Distinctiveness now comes either from one genuinely subject-specific idea executed at 60 fps on a mid-range phone, or from disciplined restraint, rarely from adding effects. Second, the leverage in AI-assisted design lies upstream of generation and downstream of it, not in the prompt's adjectives: encode the system as tokens the model must use, require a written plan reviewed against the generic default before any code, and spend the critique pass on the functional states (errors, empty screens, focus, loading) where generated interfaces are weakest and the fixes are cheapest. What remains unknown is whether any of this moves conversion; no source here measures it, so the case for craft currently rests on trust, brand and the user's experience, which for most products is reason enough.
