# Award-winning experimental and showcase websites, and the visual trends they define

Research date: 7 November 2026. Scope: 2023 to 2026. Anything older is flagged as historical.

## Which sites won the top honours in 2023 to 2026, and which studios made them?

### Takeaway
A small group of studios dominates the top prizes: abeto, Lusion, Immersive Garden, Resn, The First The Last, Louis Paquet, OFF+BRAND, Daybreak Studio and Bruno Simon. Year-level winners are almost all real-time 3D or heavily art-directed motion sites. Awwwards Site of the Year went to Lusion v3 (2023), Igloo Inc by abeto (2024) and Lando Norris by OFF+BRAND (2025). The 2026 Site of the Year has not been announced yet, as Awwwards announces it in February of the following year.

## What are Awwwards' scoring criteria, how are they weighted, and what do jury comments say?

### Takeaway
Awwwards weights Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20% and Content 10%. At least 18 jurors score each site, and the three outlying scores are dropped. A separate developer jury awards the Developer Award. Jurors say they reward a single strong art-directed idea executed with smooth motion on real devices. They penalise bolted-on effects, jank, weak mobile and ignored reduced-motion settings.

### Cited Findings
- Weights: Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10% ([Awwwards evaluation system](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)).
- Process: approved sites go to at least 18 jury members. The system automatically removes the three scores furthest from the average. Voting runs five days, and only votes from validated Professional users count. A high jury score plus at least 10 PRO user votes can win Site of the Day early ([Awwwards evaluation system](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)).
- Thresholds: an Honorable Mention needs a jury score of 6.5 or more. Site of the Day goes to the highest-scoring sites of the day. The Developer Award goes to SOTD winners that score above 7 from the developer jury. Site of the Month: the jury re-reviews the eight highest-scoring sites of the month, and the site with the most user votes gets extra weight ([Awwwards evaluation system](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)).
- Hon Tran is an Awwwards juror and two-time Independent of the Year nominee. He writes that Site of the Day "typically" needs a score in the mid-to-high 8s. He also says "Design and Usability together are 70% of your score", so creativity is a differentiator, not a substitute ([Hon Tran, Awwwards judging criteria](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)).
- Tran's description of each criterion:
  - Design: a consistent typographic system across breakpoints, deliberate negative space, restrained colour, and "one strong idea carried through". Piled-on effects read as "lack of control".
  - Usability: a first-time visitor should orient quickly, and a hero that stutters "reads as broken, not premium". Keyboard navigation, focus states and reduced-motion handling count.
  - Creativity: originality "with intent", meaning an idea that could not be moved to another brand. Generic creative-development tropes are discounted.
  - Content: placeholder copy is penalised, and content often breaks ties.

  ([Hon Tran, Awwwards judging criteria](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria))
- Tran names a Mobile Excellence track scored against Google's mobile criteria, with a 70/100 qualifying bar ([Hon Tran, Awwwards judging criteria](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)).
- Tran lists the common reasons sites score low: jank, desktop-only thinking, effects without a concept, placeholder content, missing focus states or reduced-motion handling, and slow first load ([Hon Tran, Awwwards judging criteria](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)).
- In a second article, Tran says jurors reward:
  - art direction where "the static frames still look intentional without motion"
  - choreographed transitions with meaning
  - smooth frame rates on mid-range devices
  - a reduced-motion fallback.

  He says hard cuts or plain fades, bolted-on effects and heavy 3D that drops frames "typically cap a site in the mid-7s" ([Hon Tran, best award-winning websites 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). Tran built the sites he showcases, so this is self-reported.
- Published jury comments: a juror praised Igloo Inc for pairing an immersive 3D experience with easy scroll navigation. The Developer SOTY page calls it "an immersive experience" and "pure art" ([Awwwards SOTY 2024](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards-2024/site-of-the-year); [Developer SOTY 2024](https://awwwards.com/annual-awards-2024/developer-site-of-the-year)).

### Inferences
- The 40/30 split means usability failures cannot be offset by spectacle at the Honorable Mention or SOTD level. Year-level winners, however, are chosen by vote from monthly winners, and they skew heavily towards spectacle (3D and WebGL).
- The trimmed-mean jury score rewards broad agreement. Polarising experimental work is structurally disadvantaged against polished, coherent work.

### Gaps
- The Awwwards developer jury's sub-criteria and their weights are not reproduced on the evaluation page. I recall them as semantics/SEO, animations, accessibility, web performance optimisation, responsive design and markup, but did not verify this here.
- I found no official Awwwards breakdown of per-criterion scores for the SOTY winners.

## Which techniques recur, and which are trending up or now considered dated?

### Takeaway
The award-level default stack is GSAP plus Lenis smooth scroll, scroll-driven storytelling, and Three.js or WebGL for hero scenes. Animated and kinetic typography, intro animations, custom cursors and inventive navigation sit on top of that. Rising: WebGPU, native View Transitions, CSS-generated grain and noise as a cheap alternative to WebGL depth, bento grids in product and marketing sites, and a brutalist counter-movement. Faded or restrained: heavy glassmorphism, and decorative WebGL on sites where the brand is not itself the experience.

### Cited Findings
- Orpetron's tags across its 2025 winners:
  - "Animation on scroll" and "Intro animation" appear on most entries.
  - Kinetic and animated type is frequent.
  - GSAP and Lenis are among the most-listed technologies.
  - Three.js and WebGL are common in the ambitious entries.
  - Custom cursors, creative menus and designed 404 pages recur.
  - Dark and minimal palettes appear often even on highly animated sites.

  ([Orpetron Design Legends of 2025](https://orpetron.com/blog/design-legends-of-2025-showcasing-the-best-websites-of-the-year/))
- Lenis, the smooth-scroll library by Studio Freight (now Darkroom), was itself an Orpetron 2025 Site of the Month ([Orpetron](https://orpetron.com/blog/design-legends-of-2025-showcasing-the-best-websites-of-the-year/)). Anime.js's site was Awwwards SOTM in May 2025 ([Awwwards SOTM](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_month/)). Animation-library sites now win top awards in their own right.
- Recent award sites and the techniques Tran lists for them:
  - By-Kin (https://by-kin.com/), Next.js, GSAP and Strapi: editorial typography, weighted smooth scroll and restrained transitions.
  - Iventions: a Three.js spotlight 3D scene with GSAP-paced, scroll-guided reveals.
  - Mat Voyce (https://matvoyce.tv): kinetic, scroll-driven letter animation, nominated for GSAP Site of the Year 2025.
  - Uncommon Studio: broken grid with GSAP section transitions.
  - Minh Pham: a GSAP motion system over Three.js.

  ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026))
- WebGPU: Codrops profiles Forged.build, a studio site that runs entirely on WebGPU, with compute shaders as the main difference from WebGL. Codrops' Creative Hub lists many WebGPU demos, such as page transitions and water simulations ([Codrops, Forged.build and the WebGPU revolution](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/10/20/from-garage-to-browser-forged-build-and-the-webgpu-revolution/); [Codrops 2025 demo highlights](https://tympanus.net/codrops/hub/2025-demo-highlights/)).
- View Transitions: same-document View Transitions reached Baseline in 2025 ([OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/frontend-trends-shaped-web-2025/)). Codrops covered Bramus Van Damme's Google I/O 2025 talk on View Transitions and `@starting-style` ([Codrops news](https://tympanus.net/codrops/news/)).
- StudioMeyer, an agency's six-month retrospective on its own client work (not independently verified):
  - "Bento grid layouts are now the default, not the exception", with 23% more scroll depth than 12-column grids.
  - Glassmorphism "survived in navigation bars, modals and feature cards". Heavy `backdrop-filter: blur()` caused 15 to 30% FPS drops on mid-tier Android.
  - Kinetic typography "almost never ships in production" outside Awwwards and Dribbble demos.
  - "3D elements via WebGL were supposed to become standard. They did not." One Spline hero adds 800 kB to 2 MB of JavaScript.
  - Brutalism is framed as positioning against bento.

  ([StudioMeyer on DEV](https://dev.to/studiomeyer_io/web-design-trends-2026-what-actually-held-up-after-six-months-23p8))
- Trend articles report that designers create physical depth with CSS noise texture and film grain to avoid WebGL processor cost. They recommend kinetic type as a single hero moment that settles to a static state, and say sites must work without WebGL. Envato frames 2026 around "kinetic type, broken grids and the return of visual personality" ([Envato Elements trends](https://elements.envato.com/learn/web-design-trends); [Fireart: Tactile Brutalism](https://fireart.studio/blog/the-best-web-design-trends/); [Lovable trends 2026](https://lovable.dev/guides/website-design-trends-2026)). These are vendor blogs.

### Inferences
- Up: real-time 3D as the core concept, not as a decorative hero (Igloo Inc, Lusion, Oryzo AI, Bruno Simon). Other risers are WebGPU, native CSS View Transitions and scroll-driven animations, editorial and type-led layouts (Unseen Studio, Obys, By-Kin), and textured "tactile" surfaces through grain and noise.
- Plateaued or dated: decorative Spline blobs and generic 3D heroes, heavy glassmorphism, and plain fade transitions. Kinetic type used page-wide also reads as dated, because jurors explicitly discount generic creative-development tropes. Smooth scroll is now table stakes in award work, not a differentiator.
- Bento grids are a mainstream SaaS and marketing pattern, not an award-winning one. None of the Awwwards SOTY or SOTM winners above is known for a bento layout.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative source on how often specific techniques appear (for example, the share of SOTDs using Three.js or Lenis). Orpetron's tags are the closest thing.
- I found no authoritative source on custom cursors or variable fonts being "dated". Practitioner sentiment exists but was not captured here.

## What do curated galleries (Godly, Land-book, Lapa Ninja, SiteInspire, Minimal Gallery, Httpster) show as dominant aesthetics in 2025 to 2026?

### Takeaway
I found no gallery publishing an analysis of its own dominant aesthetic. The galleries can only be characterised by their curatorial focus. Godly favours animated, WebGL and type-led interactive sites. Minimal Gallery and SiteInspire favour restraint. Httpster favours experimental work.

### Cited Findings
- Godly (https://godly.website) was founded in July 2021 and is curated by Rejiggle. It presents sites as short videos and focuses on high-end interactivity, 3D and WebGL, distinctive typography and modern grids. Counts of listed sites disagree between sources, from over 1,000 to 6,213 ([Godly info](https://godly.website/info); [UIUX Showcase](https://uiuxshowcase.com/resources/godly-website/); [Wbcom Designs, March 2026](https://wbcomdesigns.com/best-websites-design-inspiration/)).
- Minimal Gallery has run since 2013 and curates minimalist sites. Httpster is described as a showcase of "daring, often experimental web design" ([Minimal Gallery about](https://minimal.gallery/about/); [Inspo AI](https://www.inspoai.io/blogs/godly-alternatives)).

### Inferences
- Combining the curatorial focuses with the award winners above, the 2025 to 2026 showcase aesthetic splits into two camps. The first is immersive, dark-ground, motion-heavy WebGL work (Godly, Awwwards SOTY). The second is light, editorial, type-led minimalism with generous whitespace and restrained motion (Minimal Gallery, SiteInspire, CSSDA winners such as Dropbox Brand and Exat Typeface). This is an inference, not a measured finding.

### Gaps
- I found no trend reports or data from Godly, Land-book, Lapa Ninja, SiteInspire or Httpster on dominant 2025 to 2026 styles. Answering this properly needs manual sampling of each gallery's recent front pages.
- I found no Smashing Magazine, Creative Bloq or It's Nice That trend pieces in the searches run.

## What are the common criticisms of award sites, and how do the best balance spectacle with usability?

### Takeaway
The standing criticism is that award juries reward spectacle over performance, accessibility and conversion. Awwwards' Usability score has been shown to miss real accessibility errors. The best sites put spectacle in service of one idea, keep navigation simple, hold frame rate on mid-range phones, and provide reduced-motion and non-WebGL fallbacks.

### Cited Findings
- Greenspector measured the 10 Awwwards "Mobile Excellence" nominees. It found betterup.com had many accessibility errors yet still received a very high Usability score, and argued that objective measurement should temper accessibility and performance claims. The publication date is unclear and likely predates 2023, so treat it as possibly historical ([Greenspector](https://blog.greenspector.com/en/analysis_sites_nominated_mobile_excellence_awwwards/)).
- A Hacker News commenter who worked at a studio chasing Awwwards wins said flashiness "is way more important than performance" there, whether in UX, conversions or load times. This is anecdotal ([Hacker News via hn.svelte.dev](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/44221430)).
- Eric Bailey reviewed the accessibility of Awwwards Best of 2020 nominees. That is historical, and I did not retrieve the findings ([search result reference](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)).
- WebGL cost: one Spline hero scene adds 800 kB to 2 MB of JavaScript. Heavy `backdrop-filter` blur costs 15 to 30% FPS on mid-tier Android ([StudioMeyer](https://dev.to/studiomeyer_io/web-design-trends-2026-what-actually-held-up-after-six-months-23p8)).
- Kinetic typography can break for users with reduced-motion settings. Sources recommend that it plays once or on scroll, then settles to a static state ([Envato Elements](https://elements.envato.com/learn/web-design-trends); [Lovable](https://lovable.dev/guides/website-design-trends-2026)).
- Jurors' own view of the balance: performance on real mid-range devices, reduced-motion fallbacks and fast first load are scored. Jank "costs more Usability than it earns in Creativity" ([Hon Tran, judging criteria](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)).
- Jury praise for Igloo Inc names the balance directly: an immersive 3D experience paired with easy scroll navigation ([Awwwards SOTY 2024](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards-2024/site-of-the-year)).
- CSSDA gave Dropbox Brand both Website of the Year 2025 and Best UX. A site can win the top honour on a systematised, legible brand-guidelines experience rather than on WebGL spectacle ([CSSDA 2025 WOTY](https://www.cssdesignawards.com/blog/2025-website-of-the-year-winners/430/)).

### Inferences
- Balancing patterns visible across the winners:
  - Navigation that is a single linear scroll, which keeps orientation trivial even when the visuals are complex (Igloo Inc).
  - Motion concentrated in one hero idea, with calmer content sections below.
  - Static frames that still work as layouts.
  - Reduced-motion and non-WebGL fallbacks.
- For a developer, "award-level" means a distinctive art-directed concept, motion choreography with continuous transitions instead of hard cuts, a consistent type system, and real-content polish. All of it must hold 60 fps on a mid-range phone.
- Average sites fail on concept and cohesion more than on technique. Using GSAP, Lenis or Three.js does not on its own lift a site above the mid-7s.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2024 to 2026) systematic audit of Awwwards SOTY or SOTM winners for Core Web Vitals or WCAG conformance. Running Lighthouse or axe against the URLs above would fill this gap.
- I found no published Awwwards jury comments criticising specific winners.
